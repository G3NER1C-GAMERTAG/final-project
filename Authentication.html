Beyond Passwords

Methods of Authentication

Modern security goes far beyond passwords. Here are the key authentication methods used in real-worlds systems - and how combining them creates much stronger protection.

Smart Cards

Something You Have

Cards with an embedded chip that automatically recognized by an authenticating device. Proximity readers can detect them in a pocket or purse without physical contact.

Digital Certificate

Something You Have

Servers use certificates to authenticate with browsers (The 🔒 in your address bar). Issued by trusted third-party Certificate Authorities (CAs).

Biometrics

Something You Are

Uses measurable physical characteristics: Fingerprint, Retina scan, Voice, or even typing speed. Disney World uses finger biometrics for multi-day pass verification.

Hardware Tokens (OTP)

Something You Have

A physical device that generates a new One-Time Password (OTP) every 60 seconds. Since each OTP is used exactly once, intercepting it is useless to an attacker.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Know + Have

Combines two factors - such as password PLUS a PIN sent to your phone (loke Google 2-Step Verification). One stolen factor alone is useless.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Two or More Factors

Uses two or more different authentication factors. The golden standard for account security - combining know, have, and/or are dramatically reduces risk of breach.

Single Sign-On (SSO)

One Login, Many Resources

One authentication grants access to many servers or services. Used widely in schools and businesses to reduce password fatigue while maintaining security.

Passphrases

Something You Know

A string of multiple words offers more security than a complex password AND it's easiier to remember.
Example: "The 3 little pigs say do not forget the bacon!"

Top 3 Ways to Stop Password Attacks

1. Use Strong Passwords

Long passwords with mixed characters won't be in a dictionary and take too long to crack. 12+ characters, all character types.

2. Enable Account Lockout

Lock accounts after 3-5 failed attempts. This stops all automated brute force, dictionary, and spraying attacks cold.

3. Enable MFA/2FA

Even if a password is stolen, an attacker still cannot lon in without your second facter. Enable it everywhere you can.
